Sinking funds calculator

A sinking fund turns a big cost you can see coming (car insurance, holiday gifts, a new set of tires) into a small amount set aside from every paycheck. Enter each cost, when it is due and what you already have: the calculator counts the paydays you really have left before each due date and tells you what to move from each paycheck, fund by fund.

Amounts per paycheck are rounded up to the cent, so each fund reaches its target on time or a few cents above. Nothing you type leaves this page.

How the amount per paycheck is worked out

For each fund: (cost minus what is already saved) divided by the number of paydays from your next payday up to and including the due date. A payday that falls on the due date counts, one after it does not. If no payday is left before the due date, the fund is flagged: the gap has to come from money on hand.

Catch-up amount and steady amount

Starting a fund late costs more per paycheck than keeping it going. For a cost that comes back every year (or every 6 months), the calculator also shows the steady amount: the full cost spread over the paydays of the next cycle, counted on your real calendar. A biweekly year has 26 paydays most of the time and 27 in some years, so the steady amount is not always cost / 26.

Monthly view

The table adds up what leaves your paychecks each calendar month for the next 12 months. Months with an extra paycheck (3 biweekly paydays, or 5 weekly ones) show a higher total: that is the same amount per paycheck, not a mistake.

Limits

No interest is added (a high-yield savings account would only help). Costs are taken at today's price. Weekly and biweekly paydays move in steps of 7 or 14 days from the next payday you enter; "once a month" keeps the same day of the month (the last day when the month is shorter).

Keep it on paper

To track each fund by hand (yearly costs list with the amount per month, 12 deposits per fund, a spending log and a year summary), there is a printable sinking funds tracker.
